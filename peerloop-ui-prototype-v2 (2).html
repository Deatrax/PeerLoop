What this is showing

Tap through the phone. Every screen here maps to a section of the implementation spec.

  • 01

    Home is cross-hub, Hub is single-hub. Your own requests from every course live on one screen with a colour chip; knowledge and members stay strictly separated per hub.

  • 02

    The hub selector sits under the compose box. Same pattern as a model switcher — always visible, one tap, never a blocking dialog. The AI can suggest a different hub if it spots another course code, but never switches for you.

  • 03

    Fetch-first. "Check for an answer", not "Post". A high-confidence hit resolves the request and notifies nobody.

  • 04

    Pinned shows 4, expands to all. The CR controls the order; everything else lives one tap away, grouped by category. PeerLoop stores the link, never the file.

  • 05

    Answers appear by themselves. Resolve a request and it becomes a card in "Already answered" — amber dot until a CR or teacher verifies it.

  • 06

    Two escalation axes, visible in the stepper. Thread A widened peers → batch → CR. Thread B skipped peers entirely because it needed authority, not knowledge.

  • 07

    Helping tab, not a leaderboard. It's how a willing helper finds work without turning contribution into a public score.

  • 01

    The queue is ordered by what only the CR can do. Approvals first, then escalations, then answers to verify.

  • 02

    "Stalling" is the highest-leverage panel. Requests about to widen to 38 people. Answering one here prevents 38 notifications.

  • 03

    The AI proposes, the CR disposes. Every approval card shows the drafted payload and why it was raised. Nothing above the CR happens unattended.

  • 04

    Timers are editable, with guardrails. Three presets for the common case, per-stage hours behind Advanced, a 20-minute floor, and edits apply to new requests only — so a slider can't retro-fire twenty escalations.

  • 05

    Urgency changes the clock, not the path. "Today" halves every window; "Blocking" cuts it to a sixth and pings the CR in parallel. Students get a weekly quota so urgency stays meaningful.

  • 06

    Pins have a visible cutoff. The reorder list shows exactly where students stop seeing items.

  • 07

    Knowledge doesn't wait for a question. "Add knowledge" lets a rep write an answer or pin a link before anyone has asked — that's how a hub gets seeded in week one, when fetch-first has nothing to fetch from. Authored cards publish verified, with the rep's name on them.

  • 08

    The hub switcher is in every header. A rep running two courses can jump between them from Queue, Requests, Knowledge or Settings without going back to a home screen — and lands on the same screen in the other hub.

  • 01

    Designed for weekly attention, not daily triage. If a teacher has to check this every morning, the product has failed.

  • 02

    Everything on "Needs you" was approved by a class rep. That's the structural promise — the AI escalates to peers and to the rep, never past them.

  • 03

    Recurring questions is the real payoff. Six students asking the same thing in nine days is a course defect, and one tap turns the answer into permanent verified knowledge.

  • 04

    "Answer & publish" in one tap. A teacher's reply becomes a verified card immediately, so the next student never has to ask.

  • 05

    The resolution breakdown is the pilot metric. 26% answered from the hub and 38% by peers is the number that proves the loop works — the teacher bar should stay small.

  • 06

    Lock, and a kill switch. The teacher can freeze the pace and switch the agent off entirely without losing any data.

  • 01

    The department creates hubs, the teacher names the rep, the rep imports the roster. One chain of authority, so there's never a duplicate hub for the same course.

  • 02

    A second course exists for a reason. One real pilot hub plus one seeded hub proves the switcher, the cross-hub home feed and the department view without inventing fake users in the live class.

  • 03

    Cross-course patterns are the institutional value. "Where do I submit" appearing in four courses is a department problem, and it's invisible from inside any single course.

  • 04

    Red usually means a missing class rep. The escalation ladder has a hole in it, not a broken product.

  • 05

    Aggregated and anonymised. The department sees patterns and workloads, never who asked what.

  • 06

    Everything is auditable. Every state change and every AI call is exportable — that's what makes an agentic system defensible to an institution.
